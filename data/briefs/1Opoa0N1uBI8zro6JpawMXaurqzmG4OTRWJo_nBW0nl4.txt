FAQ Section
Q1: What is llms.txt and how is it different from robots.txt? llms.txt is a plain text file placed at your domain root (yourdomain.com/llms.txt) that provides a structured guide for AI crawlers. While robots.txt tells search engines what not to crawl, llms.txt tells AI systems what to prioritize and how to understand your site's content hierarchy. It uses simple markdown formatting with headings, descriptions, and links.

Q2: Do I need llms.txt if my crypto site already ranks well on Google? Yes. Google rankings and AI visibility are increasingly separate systems. A site can rank #1 on Google but be completely ignored by ChatGPT and Perplexity. llms.txt specifically helps AI crawlers understand your site structure, which traditional SEO signals don't address.

Q3: How long should a crypto llms.txt file be? Keep it focused: 10-15 of your most important pages. The goal is to provide a curated guide, not an exhaustive sitemap. Prioritize your canonical explainer, technical documentation, risk disclosures, and API references. Avoid listing every blog post or marketing page.

Q4: Does llms.txt actually improve AI citations? While llms.txt alone won't guarantee citations, it removes a significant barrier. Without it, AI crawlers must guess which of your scattered pages are most important. With it, you explicitly direct AI systems to your most authoritative content, significantly improving the accuracy and consistency of how AI describes your project.

Q5: How often should I update my llms.txt? Update it whenever you make significant content changes: new product launches, major documentation updates, new audit publications, or architectural changes to your site. At minimum, review it quarterly. An outdated llms.txt is worse than no llms.txt because it directs AI crawlers to stale content.
