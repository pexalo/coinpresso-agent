FAQ Section
Q1: Why are comparison pages so effective for AI citations? AI engines prioritize comparison pages because they provide structured, objective information that directly answers user queries. When someone asks "Uniswap vs SushiSwap," AI engines need a source that presents both sides fairly with clear data points. A well-structured comparison page is the easiest content for an LLM to extract and reformulate into an answer.

Q2: Should I compare my own crypto project against competitors? Yes, but with extreme objectivity. AI engines detect and penalize biased comparisons. The most effective approach is to present factual differences (fees, features, supported chains) without declaring a "winner." Paradoxically, the more neutral your comparison, the more likely AI engines will cite it and the more trust you build with readers.

Q3: What format works best for crypto comparison tables? Use clean HTML tables with: clear column headers (Feature, Project A, Project B), binary data where possible (Yes/No, checkmarks), objective metrics (numbers, percentages), and consistent formatting. Avoid subjective ratings (stars, scores) unless sourced from third-party aggregators.

Q4: How many comparison pages should a crypto project have? Create comparison pages for every major competitor and alternative in your category. A DeFi lending protocol should have pages comparing itself to each major competitor, plus a "roundup" page comparing all options. Aim for 5-10 comparison pages as a starting point, each targeting a specific "[Your Brand] vs [Competitor]" query.

Q5: How often should I update crypto comparison pages? At minimum monthly, and immediately when competitors change fees, add features, or face security incidents. AI engines strongly favor fresh content for financial comparisons. Include a visible "Last Updated" date on the page to signal freshness to both users and AI crawlers.
