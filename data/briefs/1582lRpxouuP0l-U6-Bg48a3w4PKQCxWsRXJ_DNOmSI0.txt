FAQ Section

Q1: Does E-E-A-T apply differently to crypto sites than traditional finance sites? Yes. Traditional finance sites prove E-E-A-T through regulatory licenses, corporate credentials, and media coverage. Crypto sites must prove it through on-chain transparency (proof-of-reserves), code quality (audit reports), and community validation (developer adoption, governance participation). AI engines are learning to recognize these Web3-native trust signals.

Q2: How can a pseudonymous crypto team demonstrate Expertise? Pseudonymous teams can demonstrate expertise through verifiable GitHub contribution history, published technical research, smart contract audit results, and protocol performance data. The key is creating machine-readable evidence of technical competence that doesn't require personal identity disclosure.

Q3: Do security audits improve AI search visibility? Yes. Security audits from recognized firms (Certik, Trail of Bits, OpenZeppelin) serve as powerful trust signals that AI engines recognize. Publishing audit results in structured, accessible formats (not just PDFs) significantly improves how AI engines evaluate your protocol's trustworthiness.

Q4: How does on-chain data contribute to E-E-A-T? On-chain data provides verifiable "Experience" signals: real TVL, actual transaction volumes, genuine user counts. When presented in formats AI engines can parse (tables, structured data), this data proves your protocol has real-world usage and isn't vaporware.

Q5: Can a new crypto project build E-E-A-T quickly? Building genuine E-E-A-T takes time, but you can accelerate it by: publishing comprehensive technical documentation, securing a security audit before launch, getting cited in established crypto media, and creating educational content that demonstrates deep expertise in your specific niche.
