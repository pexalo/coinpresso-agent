2. SERP Gap
Generic crisis pieces blur exploits and de-pegs and race to write a statement. They rarely separate verified facts from reassurance, technical cause, recovery promises, and legal conclusions.
3. Unique Coinpresso Angle
Use a two-track communications protocol: an evidence ledger for verified incident facts and a separate decision log for unverified, legal, or recovery-sensitive statements. The article is about avoiding harmful overstatement, not about operational incident response.
4. Recommended Article Structure
Section
Suggested H2

1
Exploit, de-peg, and outage are not the same crisis

2
Activate one source of truth

3
Track one of three statement types
Separate verified facts, active uncertainty, and forward-looking commitments.

4
What not to say early
Avoid “funds are safe,” attribution, root-cause, recovery, or timeline claims without evidence and approvals.

5
The first update template
Cover scope, known impact, user action if any, next-update time, and correction channel.

6
Coordinate technical, legal, and communications teams
Set approval roles and preserve a decision log.

7
Conclusion and FAQ
Include a tabletop-exercise checklist.
