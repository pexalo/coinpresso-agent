2. SERP Gap
Agency listicles judge providers by outlet count and promised placements. They rarely show buyers how to verify the difference between a wire service, sponsored inventory, genuine media relations, and a bundled strategy.
3. Unique Coinpresso Angle
Evaluate the work product, not the publisher-logo spreadsheet. The article offers a buyer’s due-diligence checklist for scope, evidence, media process, placement disclosure, sample reporting, and contract language.
4. Recommended Article Structure
Section

What the article must cover

1
Know what you are buying

2
The seven questions to ask
Ask about story development, target rationale, sample reports, disclosures, follow-up, approvals, and crisis scope.

3
Audit sample placements
Check byline, label, URL, canonical, links, publication context, and whether a reporter was involved.

4
Red flags in promises and pricing
Flag “guaranteed editorial coverage,” secret outlet lists, dofollow promises, and vague deliverables.

5
What a useful PR report looks like
Include pitches sent, rationale, responses, placement type, disclosure, learning, and next action.

6
Build a balanced scope
Combine announcement distribution with earned-media preparation when appropriate.

7
Conclusion and FAQ
Give a vendor-comparison scorecard.

Do not accuse an agency of misconduct solely because it offers wire distribution.
