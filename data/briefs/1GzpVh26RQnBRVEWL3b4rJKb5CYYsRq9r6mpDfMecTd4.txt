2. SERP Landscape and Gap
The SERP is split between generic GEO explainers and copy-paste prompt lists for traders. The latter tell readers how to ask ChatGPT for technical analysis or token ideas; they do not help a Web3 brand discover the questions that determine whether it is mentioned, cited, accurately described, or recommended.

The crucial caveat is that public, keyword-style search-volume data for exact ChatGPT prompts is limited. ChatGPT Search supports conversational questions and contextual follow-ups, which makes AI query behavior less reducible to a static list of short keywords.1 Therefore, the article should not claim that its prompts are the “most searched” unless Coinpresso has a disclosed dataset. It should show readers how to build and validate a representative prompt panel from real demand signals.
3. Unique Coinpresso Angle
The prompt list is not the deliverable; the prompt-coverage model is.

Coinpresso should create a B2B Crypto Prompt Taxonomy organized by decision stage, product category, risk level, geography, and comparison intent. The article then explains how to source prompts, test them repeatedly across models, score brand coverage, and refresh the panel. This avoids becoming another trader-prompt list and directly supports GEO planning.
4. Why This Article Can Win
The article can rank because it satisfies list intent while delivering a rigorous methodology. Generative engines can synthesize information from multiple sources, so a prompt may trigger several reformulated searches rather than map neatly to one keyword.2 Gemini’s official grounding documentation, for example, describes a workflow in which the model can generate one or multiple search queries before producing a cited answer.3

The piece must distinguish observed user language from editor-created test prompts. It should disclose the source of every prompt cluster and avoid presenting anecdotal screenshots as market-wide behavior.

7. FAQ Section
Can we see exact ChatGPT search volume for crypto prompts? Explain that exact public prompt-volume data is generally not equivalent to conventional keyword tools; use multiple first-party and public demand signals and disclose the methodology.

How many prompts should a crypto GEO audit include? Recommend coverage based on category complexity and decision paths rather than a universal count.

Should branded prompts be included? Yes, because they reveal factual accuracy and reputation issues; unbranded prompts reveal discovery and competitive visibility.

How often should the list be refreshed? Suggest monthly monitoring for priority clusters and quarterly taxonomy reviews, with faster updates after product, regulatory, or market changes.

What should a team do when a prompt produces a wrong answer? Trace the cited or likely source, correct canonical information, strengthen evidence, and retest across multiple runs instead of trying to manipulate the prompt.
